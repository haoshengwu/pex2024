#### 1. The Power EXhaust (PEX) problem

For nuclear fusion reactor, a significant amount of power will goes into the Scape-Off Layer (SOL) region. The power mainly transport along the magnetic field line and finally arrive at the divertor targets. The power flux at the divertor targets, without any protection, can be ~50 $MW m^{-2}$. This value far exceeds the enginnering limitaion ~10 $MW m^{-2}$ leading to what is known as the power exhaust (PEX) problem. Over the past thirty years, considerable efforts have been made through both experiments and modellings try to address this challenge. In this workshop, we will look at is from the modelling point of view.

#### 2. Experiments as a backgroud

The PEX related experimental values are introduced, including: <br>
1) This is the power crossing the Last Closed Flux Surface (LCFS) into the SOL region. The majority of it will eventually reach the divertor target. It is determined by the heating power, radiation power, and fusion power.<br>
2) During the operation of a fusion reactor, the fuel D and T are injected by the gas puffing system to sustain the DT reaction. To solve the PEX problem, impurities such as neon or argon are also injected to increase radiation.<br>
3) Before starting the fusion reactor, a vacuum is required to eliminate all oxygen. In addition, impurities generated during the DT fusion reaction, such as helium, and intentionally injected impurities must be removed from the reactor to maintain the DT reaction.<br>
4) In fusion reactors, there are many diagnostic systems used to measure the parameters of plasma and neutral particles. For example, electron density and electron temperature.<br>

#### 3. Magnetic field
In the PEX workshop, the nuclear fusion reactor is based on the tokamak, which is a magnetic confinement device. There are also inertial confinement fusion devices, but they are beyond our scope. For magnetic confinement devices, the most important aspect is the magnetic field. The magnetic field consists of two main components: the poloidal magnetic field and the toroidal magnetic field. The poloidal magnetic field is created by the plasma itself, along with the poloidal field coils. The toroidal magnetic field is generated by the toroidal field coils.

#### 4. Plasma transport
Plasma transport includes two parts: parallel transport along the magnetic field and cross-field transport, which is perpendicular to magnetic surface.
