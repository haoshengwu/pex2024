This page summarizes the main content of the Lecture 3 and can be used as a recap.<br>

#### 1. power flux

In the simple picture adopted in this lecture, power crosses the separatrix at the outer mid-plane (OMP) and then is transported mainly along magnetic field lines to the inner and outer divertor targets. ${q}_{//}$ at the OMP is subject to exponential decay with a constatnt value.

#### 2. Power width ${\lambda}_{q}$

The upstream parallel heat-flux decays approximately exponentially at the OMP; the corresponding e-folding length is called the power width (a.k.a. power decay length, heat flux width) and is denoted ${\lambda}_{q}$.<br>

Physically, ${\lambda}_{q}$ measures how narrow the power exhaust channel is. It determined by competition between the parallel transport and the perpendicular transport. Most of the power transports to the targets due to the strong parallel transport.<br>

The main approaches used in the community to estimate  ${\lambda}_{q}$ are: theoretical arguments based on drift-based SOL width; empirical scalings (e.g. Eich scaling); and complex edge plasma simulations .e.g. SOLPS-ITER.<br>

#### 3. Sheat-limited regime and conduction-limited regime. 

Two baseline regimes are used to interpret SOL behaviour. In the sheath-limited regime, the SOL is approximately isothermal along a flux tube; volumetric sinks are weak and the sheath heat transmission at the plates essentially decides ${q}_{//}$.<br>

In the conduction-limited regime, higher collisionality produces a strong parallel temperature gradient; electron heat conduction dominates the energy flow along the leg, while recycling-driven ionization inside the divertor becomes the main particle source and increases the target particle flux.<br>

In these two regimes, there are momentum balance along the magnetic field that the pressure at the upstream is equal to the one at the downstream (divertor target).

#### 4. Detachment

In experiments, there is momentum loss and a pressure drop between the outer midplane and the targets. In this situation, the plasma becomes detached from the divertor target plate, and the particle flux decreases. These processes can be triggered at high densities or with radiating impurities. During detachment, the total heat flux at the targets, including contributions from the plasma and surface recombination, decreases. This reduction is desirable for future nuclear fusion reactors. From the power exhaust perspective, the heat load at target can decrease to below 10 $MW/m^{-2}$, satisfying engineering limitations.
