#### 1. power flux

In the simple model, the power enter the Scrape-Off Layer at the Outer Midplane and than transport along the magnetic field line to the inner and outer target. ${q}_{//}$ at the OMP is subject to exponential decay with a constatnt value.

#### 2. Power width ${\lambda}_{q}$

The constant value  ${\lambda}_{q}$ above is named as the power width (power decay length, heat width etc.). It determined by competition between the parallel ∥ transport and  the perpendicular ⊥ transport. Most of the power transports to the targets due to the strong parallel transport. The power width is the width of the channel through which the exhausted power from the core that enters the SOL travels and hits the material surface. 

#### 3. Sheat-limited regime and conduction-limited regime. 

The sheath-limited regime is a state in which the Scrape-Off Layer is approximately isothermal along each flux tube  that in the parallel direction the gradient of temperature is small. Then the plasma sheath at the target is the only important part of the edge plasma with respect to the transport of particles and power.

Increase the upstream density, the collisionality increase that the heat conduction is dominant and there is a strong gradient of temperature. There are momentum balance along the magnetic field that the pressure at the upstream is equal to the one at the downstream target.

#### 4. Detachment

In experiments, there is momentum loss and a pressure drop between the outer midplane and the targets. In this situation, the plasma becomes detached from the divertor target plate, and the particle flux decreases. These processes can be triggered at high densities or with radiating impurities. During detachment, the total heat flux at the targets, including contributions from the plasma and surface recombination, decreases. This reduction is desirable for future nuclear fusion reactors. From a power exhaust perspective, the total heat flux will decrease to below 10 $MW/m^{-2}$, satisfying engineering limitations.
