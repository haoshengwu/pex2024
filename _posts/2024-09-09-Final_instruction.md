## What to Do?

Four projects related to power exhaust modelling are provided. Each project is derived from the ongoing research from NEMO group. In other words, the projects are based on real research work. After the workshop, each student will be randomly assigned one of the four projects. Each project includes several sub-questions, and students are expected to answer these questions independently.<br>
The list containing the names of students and their corresponding project numbers will be released when we finish the workshop.<br>

## How to do?

During the hands-on sessions, students will conduct physical analyses related to power exhaust modeling. Similarly, for the final project, they will perform physical analyses to address the sub-questions associated with each project. A Jupyter Notebook template for each project will be provided within the Docker image, enabling students to conduct their analyses and answer the sub-questions.<br>

The final project submission will be in PDF format, generated directly from the student's Jupyter Notebook file. In other words, the final report will include the entire analysis process, such as relevant Python scripts and answers to the sub-questions. In general, each question does not have a fixed answer and focuses on understanding and analyzing the physical processes. The report will be evaluated by Prof. Subba.<br>

**Students should complete the final project independently and are not allowed to use any artificial intelligence tools for the project.**

**If you find the final project difficult, you can refer to the book *The Plasma Boundary of Magnetic Fusion Devices* by Peter C. Stangeby.**

## When to do?

If the student have any questions related to the final project, please contact haosheng.wu@polito.it before **January 31st**. After this date, the questions related the final projects will no longer be answered.
